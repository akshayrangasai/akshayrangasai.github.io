There's a concept that's been gaining traction in developer circles called "learning in public." The idea is straightforward: instead of consuming content silently, you share what you're learning as you go.

Blog posts, tweets, notes, half-baked tutorials — the format doesn't matter. The act of externalizing your thinking is what creates value.

## Why it works

Writing forces clarity. When you try to explain something you just learned, you quickly discover the gaps in your understanding. Those gaps are where the real learning happens.

It's also a form of documentation. Future-you will thank present-you for writing down how that obscure API works, or why you chose one approach over another.

## The uncomfortable part

The hardest part isn't the writing — it's the publishing. Putting something out there before you fully understand it feels vulnerable. What if you're wrong? What if someone who knows more corrects you?

That's actually the best-case scenario. Being corrected is faster than being quietly wrong for months.

## Getting started

You don't need a big audience. You don't even need an audience at all. Write for yourself, in public, and let the compounding effects take care of the rest.

Start with what you learned today. Keep it short. Ship it.
