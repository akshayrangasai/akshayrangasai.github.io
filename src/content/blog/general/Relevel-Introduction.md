### The job finding process is broken 

I've changed about 5 jobs in ~10 years, and as a **veteran**  job hunter, I have always found the job hunt process to be broken. I think it has 3 core problems, that nobody seems to be solving, especially for roles in tech:

1. It is painful and manual to keep track of interesting companies you'd like to be a part of
2. Keeping track of jobs you apply to, and the state it is in is impossibly difficult 
3. Collaborating with friends on the job search is super high friction

I'll explain with a few anecdotes from my life

When I moved into tech, I had a "dream" list of companies I wanted to track, and then apply whenever a job opening showed up, and at the same time, find people whom I might know at the company and get a referral if needed. These companies were either companies I knew, or companies funded by a good VC in a space I liked, or companies I found in a newsletter about fundraises or something else describing how good they are. 

Once I found these companies, most of my time is spent in copy pasting job roles from one page to a spreadsheet, going back to the right URL when I had time and applying to them. As I got emails, and time went on, I spent most of my time updating the status of whatever I'd applied to, and figuring out if anything I was applying to was working - it was very hard to figure out if I was doing something right. Most importantly, I wanted to know if a job I applied to ever closed before I even got a rejection email. 

Last, but not the least, I always had a spreadsheet shared with a bunch of friends where we added companies we wanted to join in separate tabs, and then have another spreadsheet to track our progress without sharing the actual gory details of what was happening. We shared companies for two reasons, awareness, and the hope that someone else has a contact they can put us in touch with for a referral
